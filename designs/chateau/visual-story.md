## Scroll pacing plan

All distances below are **CSS vh of pinned scroll travel**. Three viewport heights means 300 vh, not 3 CSS vh. The proposed baseline is 300 vh of exterior motion, 250 vh of foyer motion and 200 vh of motion per room. Each scene adds one 50 vh still-frame hold. Dips are included in the motion allocation. Total pinned travel is **1,400 vh, or 14 viewport heights**, followed immediately by contact content.

A hold is a scroll interval mapped to one frame, not a timed pause. Stopping the scrollbar freezes every visual, including the brain pulse, screen demonstration and copy animation. No wheel interception or forced dwell. Reading time ultimately belongs to the visitor; gate 7 will test whether these distances are comfortable.

Frame intervals are **proposed editorial targets**, not verified assets. The exterior target is 216 frames, or 12 seconds at 18 fps. Each other scene targets 180 frames, or 10 seconds. A model's available durations must be checked before submission; if the actual approved clip differs, recalculate these intervals around its observed action and holds. Do not duplicate frames or interpolate a short clip to disguise missing motion.

### Piecewise mapping and reverse behaviour

Within each row, use `t = clamp((scrollVh - startVh) / (endVh - startVh), 0, 1)`, then `frame = round(startFrame + t * (endFrame - startFrame))`. A hold has identical start and end frames. Use half-open intervals, with the final endpoint included in the last beat. Adjacent rows share their endpoint frame, so each hold is continuous in either direction.

Clip frame numbers above are local. The proposed concatenated sequence uses exterior offset 0, foyer 216, Room 1 396, Room 2 576 and Room 3 756, for 936 frames total. The cut switches to the next scene at full black. Every scene's last 10 vh fades out; the next scene's first 10 vh fades in. The first scene begins visible, and the last fade hands directly to contact. Both frame selection and dip opacity are pure functions of scroll position, so reversing reconstructs the same threshold.

Copy opacity and position also derive from the specified scroll ranges. Invisible copy and screen layers have no pointer events or tab stops. Each room's CTA moves to the end contact section; Skip tour targets that same section and places keyboard focus there. Scrolling back from contact restores the last black threshold before the room reappears.

## Composition and screen requirements

Landscape copy uses approximately x = 7–38% and y = 52–85% of the frame, adjusted per scene. Reserve the central 40–72% for important action. The foyer sphere and seated figure stay centred around x = 52%; do not solve copy placement by pushing either to an extreme edge. Keep the top 12% quiet for the wordmark and Skip tour. These are composition targets to inspect in stills, not automatic overlay coordinates.

The Room 1 projection occupies roughly x = 43–75%, y = 20–82%. Within it, the iPhone UI must remain large enough to read, with just one short question and answer. The Room 3 hero panel occupies roughly x = 42–75%, y = 25–72%; the operator stays beside it and must not cross its face during the demonstration. Use tracked corner coordinates for a real DOM plane; a fixed rectangle will not survive camera movement. Fade UI before an occlusion, withhold clip approval if the required clean window is missing, and do not fake it with text baked into the image.

Portrait is a separate composition made later from the approved references. Keep the subject or screen in the upper-middle area, approximately x = 18–82%, y = 18–53%, with quiet stone or shadow below for copy at y = 58–88%. Use a modestly sized portrait heading and reflow the UI for the available space. Keep every beat and cut aligned to landscape. If actual text or UI will not fit, revise the portrait composition and copy at review; do not shrink the desktop page to fit.

Use warm chandelier light with neutral shadows, pale limestone, dark oak and aged brass throughout. Screens may add a restrained warm-white fill. The company brain is a dark spherical object with fine brass surface light, not a literal anatomical brain. Human figures wear quiet charcoal clothing with no recognisable actor likeness. Reuse approved identity references across any related stills and clips.
